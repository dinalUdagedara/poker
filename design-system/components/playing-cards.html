Playing cards

The card is still the brightest thing in the room, which is what lets the rest of the scheme sit this dark without going murky. It is never tinted, faded or given a coloured face. The drawing is a screen card: rank and a small pip at the top left, one large pip in the bottom right.

Rules
  • The back is near-black house stock inside a brass rule — never red. Counting an opponent’s cards from across the felt is the entire job of a face-down card, so it has to stay the darkest thing out there.
  • Ranks are the grotesque, bold, stacked over a small pip at the top left, with one large pip in the bottom right. The inverted corner is gone: that was the printed-card claim, and this is a screen card.
  • Cards are opaque. Fading is applied to the hand (.hand-folded), never to each card — overlapping translucent cards show through one another and double up where they cross.
  • Overlap is allowed to bury the large pip. The rank lives in the top left, which is the corner a fan leaves open.
