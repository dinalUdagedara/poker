Playing cards

The card is the brightest thing in the room and the only object with a light surface. That is deliberate: it is what lets the rest of the scheme sit this dark without going murky, and it is why a card is never tinted, faded or given a coloured face.

Rules
  • The back is near-black house stock inside a brass rule — never red. A red back on a red table is one object, and counting an opponent’s cards from across the felt is the entire job of a face-down card.
  • Ranks are set in the didone. That printed look is most of what makes a rectangle read as a card.
  • Cards are opaque. Fading is applied to the hand (.hand-folded), never to each card — overlapping translucent cards show through one another and double up where they cross.
  • Overlap is shallow. These carry their rank in the middle, so a deep fan buries it.
